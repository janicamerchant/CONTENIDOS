# Production workflow

## Carousel

- Default to 6-7 slides.
- Slide 1: high-curiosity hook; few words; immediate relevance.
- Slides 2-5/6: one idea per slide, alternating layouts and visual devices.
- Penultimate slide: practical value, framework, checklist, comparison, or proof.
- Final slide: one primary CTA and brand signature.
- Avoid long paragraphs. Prefer statistics, diagrams, comparisons, checklists, and short statements.
- Do not repeat substantially identical layouts or subject poses.

## Narrative photography (every carousel)

A photo must tell the slide's idea without reading the text. A portrait that only shows a face is not enough.

- Read the slide's headline, then decide the scene: who is doing what, where, and what object or moment proves the idea. Example: for "Your lead didn't wait. They bought from someone else." show an insurance agent at night staring at a phone full of unanswered WhatsApp messages, or a customer happily signing with a competitor while the agent's missed-call notification lights up.
- Choose the person with creative freedom: Janica Merchant (official references) when she can be *doing* something related (presenting the dashboard, reviewing leads, coaching a team), the public figure of the news (real photos only), or a hyperrealistic person living the situation. Objects and places are valid protagonists too: a phone with 14 unread chats, an empty chair in front of a desk, a calendar full of appointments.
- Never place Janica or any person as generic decoration. If her presence doesn't explain the idea, use another scene.
- Photo on most slides: at least 5 of 7 slides carry a photograph (full-bleed, side, inset, or band). At most one or two slides may be purely typographic, and never two in a row.
- Layer type and image: oversized headlines partially behind the subject (using a background-removed cutout of the same photo), numbers crossing over objects, text on panels that overlap the photo. Vary the crop and position slide to slide.
- For every slide, write two things: a short art direction in the brief language, and a generation prompt in English (subject, action, setting, emotion, framing, light, "hyperrealistic editorial photograph, natural skin texture, no text, no logos").

## Image generation

- Generate 4:5 at high resolution for Instagram feed.
- Provide exact text and instruct the generator not to add extra words.
- Use logo, face, product, vehicle, or location references as separate inputs when available.
- Maintain the brand's palette and typography hierarchy across the set while varying composition.
- Inspect all output text. Regenerate or edit any slide with spelling errors or identity drift.

## Video and reel

- Establish duration, platform, aspect ratio, language, audio/voiceover, and CTA.
- Build a shot list before generating multi-scene video.
- Keep a consistent character reference across scenes.
- Use real product screenshots or approved vehicle/product references when accuracy matters.
- Do not add music or voiceover when the brief forbids it.

## Research

- Prefer official primary sources for current platform policies, vehicle specifications, and offers.
- State uncertainty rather than converting an estimate into a universal fact.
- Separate attention-grabbing hook copy from the factual clarification inside the carousel.

## Quality checklist

- Correct brand and language.
- Exact logo and approved palette.
- Correct people and facial identity.
- Correct model, year, product, price, and dates.
- Text readable on mobile; no overflow or typos.
- No private client identifiers.
- CTA matches the objective.
